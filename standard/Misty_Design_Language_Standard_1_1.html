The Misty standard

See what you see

The most elegant design language for the future.

1.1About this standard#

This document specifies the Misty design language: the principles, tokens, materials, type, iconography, motion and components that together make an interface recognisably Misty. It governs the visual language and interaction behaviour of every product that claims to use Misty, on any platform. Web is the first platform, so values are written as CSS, but every token and requirement is platform-neutral. Annex A maps them to Apple platforms, Android, Windows, Flutter and JavaScript runtimes, and Annex B covers languages, scripts and writing direction.

The reference implementation is the Misty 1.1 reference stylesheet, published as platforms/web/misty.css. These pages embed it in one self-contained HTML file with no external styles, scripts, fonts or images. These pages are rendered with that stylesheet, so every figure here is the language running live. Change the hue or the appearance from the palette in the top bar and the whole standard follows.

GEN-01Where this standard and the reference implementation disagree, this standard governs. A disagreement MUST be fixed in the implementation in the next patch release, not by reading the standard more loosely.

The standard is written for designers who draw Misty interfaces, engineers who build them, reviewers who check them and the people who maintain the language.

1.2How to read the requirements#

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 (RFC 2119 and RFC 8174) when, and only when, they appear in capitals, as shown here.

Key wordMeaning in MistyConformance
MUST, MUST NOTAn absolute requirement or prohibition.Does not conform.
SHOULD, SHOULD NOTThe default. There may be valid reasons to depart in a particular case, once the consequences are understood.Conforms if the departure and its reason are recorded.
MAYTruly optional. Products choose freely.Conforms.

Every requirement has a stable identifier made of its clause prefix and a number, such as MAT-03. Identifiers keep their meaning for the life of a major version; a removed requirement retires its number rather than passing it on. Link to a requirement with its identifier in lower case, for example #rule-mat-03.

Clauses are either Normative or Informative, as shown in the facts panel at the top of each clause. Informative clauses explain and recommend and contain no requirements. Figures, examples, notes and do-and-don’t pairs are informative wherever they appear; only the numbered requirements bind.

1.3Structure of the standard#

Thirty-two clauses in six parts, followed by two annexes. The order follows the way a Misty interface is built: principles first, then the foundations beneath every surface, the visible styles, the components assembled from them, and the patterns that combine components into pages.

1.5Conformance#

There are two levels of conformance. Misty Core covers the foundations and styles, clauses 4 to 16: a product at this level uses Misty’s tokens, layout, access rules, materials, colour, type, icons and motion, even if it builds its own controls. Misty Complete adds clauses 17 to 24 for every component the product ships. Both levels include the requirements of clauses 1 and 2. Annexes A and B add requirements at both levels for the products they describe: native implementations, and products in more than one language or writing direction.

GEN-02A product that claims conformance MUST meet every MUST and MUST NOT requirement in the clauses of its level.

GEN-03A product that departs from a SHOULD or SHOULD NOT requirement MUST record the requirement’s identifier and the reason in its design notes.

GEN-04A conformance claim MUST name the version of the standard, the level and the date of the review, for example “Misty 1.1 Complete, reviewed 2 November 2026”.

GEN-05A product MAY add components, patterns and tokens of its own. Additions MUST be built only from Misty tokens and MUST NOT change the meaning of an existing token, component or requirement.

The conformance checklist gathers every requirement in one place, with a filter by key word and a copyable review for a ticket or pull request.

2Principles

Misty begins with a lit field, not a page of panels. Every requirement in this standard follows from four principles, and when two requirements pull in different directions, the principles decide in a fixed order.

2.1Light before surface#

Content sits in an atmospheric field. Glass borrows the light behind it, so depth comes from what shows through, not from borders, gloss or stacks of grey.

A Misty screen is lit before it is divided. The field, a soft wash of the hue with a fine grain, is always the bottom layer, and every surface above it is a thin sheet that lets some of that light through. Hierarchy comes from how much of the field a surface reveals: the higher a surface floats, the more it veils.

  • Every view starts on the field.
  • Every surface is one of the four grades of glass.
  • Depth is one hairline and one soft shadow, at one of four elevation levels.

2.2One hue, many depths#

A single hue parameter tints the field, the ink, the accent and the shadows. Neutrals are never pure grey, and semantic colours never move with the theme.

Misty has no palette to choose from. It has one number. Because every tinted colour is defined in OKLCH from that number, the interface stays in tune at whatever hue a person chooses, and lightness, and with it contrast, holds steady all the way round the wheel. Only the colours that carry meaning, such as success, warning and danger, keep their own fixed hues.

  • One parameter, --m-hue, drives every theme colour.
  • Ink is tinted with the hue; Misty has no pure grey.
  • Semantic colours are fixed and never decorative.

2.3Motion shows direction#

Pages grow out of what you pressed, settle when you go back, and slide when you move sideways. Nothing moves just to look alive.

Motion answers two questions: where did this come from, and where does it go? The glass stays still and only the content changes, in the direction the person travelled. These pages use the same transitions. Open a clause from the overview and it grows in; step to the next clause in the same part and it slides; go back and it settles.

  • Four navigation transitions cover every move between views.
  • Five durations and four curves cover everything else.
  • With reduced motion, every state still reads at rest.

2.4Clarity over decoration#

One hairline, one soft shadow, no simulated thickness. If a flourish makes text harder to read or a control harder to find, it is not Misty.

Atmosphere is welcome only while it costs nothing in legibility. Text that needs reading moves onto a solid surface. Controls are quiet by default and obvious in use: one accent action per view, a visible focus ring, labels beside icons whose meaning is not universal.

  • Text on glass follows strict limits.
  • A view has at most one accent action.
  • Words are plain, specific and in sentence case.

2.5When principles disagree#

Requirements are written to agree, but a specific design can still set two of them against each other: a translucent sheet that makes a label hard to read, or a transition that slows down a task someone repeats all day. Misty settles these by a fixed priority.

  1. Access. A person can perceive, operate and understand the interface with whatever settings and assistive technology they use.
  2. Clarity. Words and controls are easy to read and easy to find.
  3. Direction. Layout and movement show where things come from and where they go.
  4. Atmosphere. Light, glass and hue.
ConflictResolutionDeciding priority
The person asks for reduced transparencyEvery surface becomes Cloud and the grain disappearsAccess over atmosphere
A long paragraph would sit on MistThe paragraph moves to CloudClarity over atmosphere
A menu is opened dozens of times a dayIt appears in 240 ms with Fluid, never a scene revealClarity over direction
Reduced motion is on during navigationViews swap instantly; position and headings still show where you areAccess over direction

PRI-01When two requirements of this standard conflict in a specific design, the requirement that serves the higher priority, in the order access, clarity, direction, atmosphere, MUST take precedence.

PRI-02Decoration that this standard does not specify, such as gradient text, glows, bevels, inner shadows, simulated thickness or parallax, MUST NOT be added to Misty surfaces.

PRI-03A new component or pattern SHOULD be expressible with existing tokens and materials. One that needs a new material, curve or colour role SHOULD be proposed as a change to the standard instead.

TOK-02Token names MUST NOT encode a raw value, such as --m-space-16px or --m-blue, so that a value can change without renaming the token.

TOK-04Component properties MUST be set to tokens or to values this standard specifies, and MUST NOT be used to introduce colours of their own.

TOK-05A product MAY define tokens of its own under its own prefix. It MUST NOT define new tokens under --m-.

4.3Theming with tokens#

Two inputs control every theme token: the data-theme attribute and the --m-hue property. Both are read where theme tokens are declared, on the root element and on any element that carries data-theme. That second place is what makes a scoped island, such as a dark preview inside a light page, possible.

html
<html lang="en" data-theme="dark" style="--m-hue: 165">
  <!-- the whole page in Sage, dark -->
  <aside data-theme="light">
    <!-- a light island inside it -->
  </aside>
</html>

TOK-06--m-hue MUST be set on the root element or on an element that carries data-theme. On any other element it has no effect, because theme tokens are computed where they are declared.

TOK-07Without a stored choice, a page MUST leave data-theme unset so that the appearance follows the system preference.

4.4Token reference#

Every token in the reference stylesheet, as declared. Theme tokens show both values. Select a name to copy it as a var().

5.2Margins and gutters#

The inline margin of every page is --m-gutter, clamp(1.125rem, 4vw, 2rem): 18 px on the smallest phones, growing with the window to 32 px. Gaps between cards come from the spacing scale, usually 12 to 16 px.

LAY-03Page content MUST keep at least --m-gutter of inline padding at every window width.

5.3Grid#

Layouts align to a fluid 12-column grid with 12 to 16 px gaps. The common divisions are full width, two thirds and one third, halves, thirds and quarters.

LAY-04Layouts SHOULD align to the 12-column grid.

LAY-05When a column becomes too narrow for its content, columns MUST stack rather than shrink text or controls below their specified sizes.

5.4Window classes#

Five window classes, taken from the reference breakpoints, describe how a layout adapts. Each class names a change of structure, not a device.

LAY-07Breakpoints for page structure SHOULD come from the five window classes. Components MAY adapt to their own container with container queries.

5.5Spacing#

Twelve steps on a 4 px base. The steps grow slowly at the small end, where components need fine control, and quickly at the large end, where sections need air.

5.6Layering#

Every layer above the content has one fixed place in the stack. Dialogs sit above all of them in the browser’s top layer.

LAY-09An overlay MUST use the layer assigned to its component. New values MUST NOT be introduced between layers without adding them to this scale.

LAY-10Modal dialogs MUST use the browser’s top layer, through a <dialog> opened with showModal().

5.7Safe areas and touch#

On devices with rounded corners and cut-outs, Misty pages declare viewport-fit=cover, then keep content and fixed controls inside env(safe-area-inset-*). The top bar and the strip above it share the same glass, so the bar reads as one sheet up to the edge of the screen.

LAY-11Content and fixed controls MUST stay clear of the safe-area insets.

LAY-12On coarse pointers, every interactive target MUST be at least 44 × 44 px. A control MAY look smaller if its hit area extends to that size.

6.1Baseline#

ACC-01Every Misty product MUST meet WCAG 2.2 at Level AA.

ACC-02The requirements in this clause are minimums. Where WCAG asks for more in a particular case, WCAG MUST be followed.

6.2Contrast#

Every ink step from 50 up meets 4.5:1 on the field at every hue, in both appearances. The table below is measured live in this browser, for the appearance and hue you are viewing now.

ACC-03Text MUST meet 4.5:1 against what is behind it, and large text, 24 px or 18.66 px bold, MUST meet 3:1.

ACC-04Ink 30 and lighter steps MUST NOT carry text, except in disabled controls.

Misty’s hairlines are deliberately quiet, at about 1.6:1 on the field, so no control depends on its hairline alone to be found. Fields have a visible label and a lighter fill; switches and checkboxes have a label and a filled accent state; buttons have a label and a fill.

ACC-05Every control MUST be identifiable without relying on a boundary below 3:1: it MUST carry a visible text label and at least one other cue, such as a fill, an icon or its position in a labelled group.

ACC-06Icons and state indicators that are needed to understand or operate a control MUST meet 3:1.

6.3Text on glass#

Translucency lowers contrast whenever something bright or busy passes behind a surface. Misty’s glass rules are accessibility rules first.

ACC-07Text MUST NOT be placed directly on Clear. Labels for Clear surfaces sit on a solid tag.

ACC-08Text longer than three lines, or text that a person reads continuously, MUST sit on Cloud or directly on the field.

ACC-09Meaning MUST survive when every surface becomes Cloud: grouping, state and hierarchy cannot depend on transparency.

Do

Set reading text on Cloud, even when it sits over a busy area.

Don’t

Put paragraphs on Clear, where anything passing behind fights the words.

6.4Focus#

The focus ring is two rings: a 2 px gap in the field colour and a 2 px ring in accent text, drawn outside the control so it never covers its label. Controls without their own ring use a 2 px accent outline offset by 3 px.

ACC-10Every interactive element MUST show a visible focus indicator when it receives keyboard focus, using --m-focus or the 2 px accent outline.

ACC-11Focus indicators SHOULD appear for keyboard focus only, through :focus-visible, and MUST NOT be removed without a replacement.

6.5Targets#

Buttons are 40 px tall and icon buttons 38 px on fine pointers. On touch, every icon-only control extends an invisible hit area to 44 × 44 px without changing how it looks.

ACC-12On coarse pointers, interactive targets MUST be at least 44 × 44 px, including the hit areas of icon-only controls.

26.2Layout#

  • In a page, actions align to the start under the last field. In a dialog, they align to the end.
  • Confirm with the action’s own word: “Save settings” leads to “Settings saved”.

29.3Remembering the choice#

The reference implementation stores two values, misty-theme as light, dark or system, and misty-hue from 0 to 360, and applies them in a small script in the head, before the first paint. When storage is unavailable, the page still works for the visit and falls back to the system appearance and the default hue next time.

head script
(() => {
  const root = document.documentElement;
  let theme = null, hue = null;
  try {
    theme = localStorage.getItem("misty-theme");
    hue = localStorage.getItem("misty-hue");
  } catch { /* storage can be blocked */ }
  if (theme === "light" || theme === "dark") root.dataset.theme = theme;
  const h = Number(hue);
  if (hue !== null && h >= 0 && h <= 360) root.style.setProperty("--m-hue", h);
})();

32.1Terms#

Accent
The one strong colour of a view, derived from the hue, used for the primary action and the current place. Colour
Aurora
The soft glows of the field, at the hue, the hue plus 55° and the hue minus 70°. Materials
Clause
A numbered part of this standard. Each page is a clause; its sections are numbered sub-clauses.
Clear
The most transparent grade of glass, for atmosphere only. Never carries text. Materials
Cloud
The solid grade: no blur, for reading surfaces and every fallback.
Edge
The single 1 px inset hairline that every glass surface has.
Elevation
One of four levels of soft shadow that say how far a surface floats. Elevation
Exit
The accelerating curve for things that leave. Motion
Field
The lit ground beneath every view: sky colour, aurora and grain, fixed while content scrolls.
Fluid
The default curve for everything that enters or moves: fast at first, with a long, soft finish.
Glass
A flat, see-through surface with a fill, a backdrop blur, one edge and one shadow.
Grade
One of the four kinds of glass: Clear, Mist, Veil and Cloud.
Grain
The fine fractal noise over the field, 4.5% in light and 6.5% in dark.
Hue
The one parameter, from 0 to 360, from which every theme colour is derived. Colour
Identity glyph
A 48-unit glyph on a tinted tile that names a place or a product. Iconography
Informative
Content that explains or recommends and contains no requirements.
Ink
The hue-tinted near-black, or near-white in dark, used at six opacity steps for text and icons. Colour
Island
An element with its own data-theme, rendered in a different appearance from the page around it.
Logical property
A CSS property named for the flow of text, such as margin-inline-start, rather than for a side of the screen, so that layouts mirror in right-to-left languages. Internationalisation
Mist
The grade for cards and panels in the flow of the page.
Native implementation
A product built with a platform’s own toolkit, such as SwiftUI, Jetpack Compose, WinUI or Flutter, rather than with HTML and CSS. Platforms
Normative
Content that contains requirements a conforming product must meet.
Provisional
Content that may change in a minor release without a deprecation period.
Pseudo-localisation
Replacing interface text with accented text 40% longer, to find layouts that clip and strings that aren’t translated. Internationalisation
Scrim
The tinted, blurred layer behind a modal dialog. Elevation
Spring
The overshooting curve for small tactile objects.
Token
A named value, a CSS custom property with the --m- prefix. Design tokens
Veil
The grade for everything that floats above content.
Window class
One of five ranges of window width, from Narrow to Wide, at which a layout changes structure. Layout

A.1Packages#

Every token is defined once, in tokens/misty.tokens.json, in the Design Tokens Format Module 2025.10. A build script turns it into a package for each platform. Each package computes the theme colours from the hue while the app runs, with the conversion browsers use, so a hue chosen on one device matches on every other.

A.2Units and fluid sizes#

One CSS pixel is one point on Apple platforms, one density-independent pixel on Android and Flutter, and one effective pixel on Windows. Rem values assume a 16 px root, so 1rem is 16 points, 16 dp or 16 epx.

Fluid tokens, such as --m-text-h1 and --m-gutter, are clamp() expressions over the window’s width. The packages evaluate the same expression against the window’s width in points or dp, then the platform scales text by the person’s text size. At four common widths:

A.3Colour#

Theme colours are defined in OKLCH from the hue. The packages convert OKLCH to sRGB with the OKLab matrices that CSS Color Module Level 4 uses and clip each channel to the sRGB range, which is what current browsers do on an sRGB display. Many accent and field colours fall outside sRGB at some hues, so a converter that maps the gamut another way drifts from the web by a visible step.

PLT-03A native implementation SHOULD compute theme colours from the hue while it runs, with the reference conversion, and SHOULD NOT ship colours baked for one hue unless the product never changes its hue.

PLT-04On displays with a wider gamut than sRGB, an implementation MAY draw the unclipped colour in Display P3, as browsers do, provided every pair in the contrast table still meets its threshold.

A.4Materials#

CSS blur lengths are standard deviations of the Gaussian blur, and Flutter’s ImageFilter.blur takes the same value. SwiftUI’s materials and WinUI’s acrylic have a fixed blur, so Apple and Windows products use the closest material and lay the grade’s tint over it. Jetpack Compose can blur a composable’s own content but not what lies behind it, so Android products draw glass as Cloud, which MAT-08 already requires wherever blur is unavailable.

A.5Type and text size#

Native products use the platform’s interface face, as TYP-01 requires on the web: SF Pro on Apple platforms, Segoe UI Variable on Windows and Roboto on Android. No package bundles a typeface. Weights carry across unchanged, so 600 is semibold everywhere.

Native platforms let people choose one text size for every app. Misty’s sizes are the defaults at the standard setting, and the platform scales them from there.

PLT-06Text SHOULD scale with the platform’s text size setting, such as Dynamic Type, font scale or text scaling, to at least 200% without clipping text that a person needs in order to act.

BInternationalisation

Misty is written in British English and set left to right, but it’s meant for every language. This annex covers writing direction, scripts and typefaces, text that grows in translation and formats that follow the reader’s locale. It gathers the language rules of clauses 9, 14 and 15 and adds what right-to-left and non-Latin scripts need.

B.1Writing direction#

Set dir on the root element for the language of the page, and on any part in a language with the other direction. The reference stylesheet uses logical properties, such as margin-inline-start, so margins, padding, borders and positions follow the direction without separate rules. Directional glyphs mirror, as ICO-10 requires, and so do the travel of a switch, the fill of sliders and progress, the arrow keys in tabs and the side the Beside transition enters from.

Show every page right to left

The text stays in English; only the layout mirrors. The preview lasts until you turn it off or reload the page.

INT-01Every page SHOULD declare its language with lang and its direction with dir on the root element, and again on any part in another language or direction.

INT-02In right-to-left layouts, everything that follows reading order SHOULD mirror: layout, navigation order, directional glyphs, the Beside transition, progress, sliders and the travel of a switch. Media controls, clocks, numeric chart axes and code SHOULD NOT mirror.

INT-03Text that always reads left to right, such as email addresses, web addresses, phone numbers, code and token names, SHOULD be isolated with dir="ltr" or bdi, so that it keeps its order inside right-to-left text.

B.2Scripts and typefaces#

Misty uses the platform’s interface face, and the browser falls back to a face that has the script. Han characters are shared by Chinese, Japanese and Korean but drawn differently in each, so the stack changes with the language tag: Japanese reaches for Japanese faces before Chinese ones, and Traditional Chinese for Traditional faces before Simplified ones.

INT-05Letter spacing SHOULD NOT be applied to scripts whose letters join or stack, such as Arabic, Devanagari and Thai. Labels that use tracking and capitals in Latin text SHOULD stand apart by weight in these scripts and in scripts without case.

INT-06Headings in scripts with marks above and below the line, such as Arabic, Thai, Devanagari and Vietnamese, SHOULD keep a line height of at least 1.3, so that the marks aren’t clipped.

B.3Growth and wrapping#

Translations are often longer than English, by a third or more in German and Finnish, and short labels can double. WRI-10 asks layouts to leave room for 40% more. Pseudo-localisation tests this before any translation exists: it swaps letters for accented ones, pads each string by 40% and brackets it, so clipped and untranslated text stands out.

INT-07Before release, every view SHOULD be checked with pseudo-localised text 40% longer than the source, in both writing directions.

INT-08Headings SHOULD allow hyphenation in the page’s language, with hyphens: auto, and long words SHOULD be allowed to break, so that compound words and long names wrap instead of overflowing.

B.4Locale formats#

WRI-08 asks for dates, times and numbers in the reader’s format. Use the platform’s formatter rather than building strings: Intl on the web and in JavaScript runtimes, Foundation’s formatters on Apple platforms, android.icu.text on Android, Windows.Globalization on Windows and the intl package in Flutter.

INT-09Dates, times, numbers, units and lists SHOULD be formatted with the platform’s locale-aware formatter, not assembled by hand.

INT-10Plurals and other grammatical variation SHOULD be written as whole messages that translators can see, such as ICU MessageFormat, rather than chosen between fragments in code, as WRI-11 requires.

B.5Translating this standard#

The text of this standard is licensed under CC BY-ND 4.0, with an additional permission for unofficial translations, as 1.7 sets out. A translation is welcome when it says at its start that it’s unofficial and that the English text governs, links to the version it translates and changes nothing beyond what translation needs.

INT-11A translation of this standard SHOULD keep requirement identifiers, token names, clause numbers and figure numbers unchanged, so that a review in one language can be checked against another.
