# Trademark policy

ZodiaCALs owns these marks, together called “the marks” below:

- the name **Misty**, as used for design languages, design systems and the documents and software that implement them;
- the Misty mark, the gradient square with a sun and three lines;
- the names of the conformance levels, **Misty Core** and **Misty Complete**.

The marks tell people that something is Misty as ZodiaCALs defines it. This policy keeps that meaning reliable, so it’s strict. It applies on top of the licences in `LICENSE.md`, which grant no trademark rights.

## Uses that need no permission

- **Factual reference.** Saying in plain text that a product is built with Misty or follows it, such as “Designed with the Misty design language”.
- **Conformance claims.** Stating a claim in the form that GEN-04 gives, such as “Misty 1.1 Core, reviewed 2 November 2026”, or with the platform as PLT-11 suggests, for a product that meets every MUST at that level. Keep the record of the review and show it to ZodiaCALs if asked.
- **Unchanged copies.** Sharing unmodified copies of the standard, or unmodified packages under their own names.
- **Unofficial translations.** Naming the standard in a translation shared under the translation permission in `LICENSE.md`.
- **Commentary.** Articles, talks, courses, reviews and comparisons that refer to Misty, including criticism.

## Uses that need written permission

- Using “Misty”, or a name that could be confused with it, in the name of a product, company, package, library, plug-in, theme, template, domain or social media account, such as “Misty UI”, “misty-react” or “Misty Kit”.
- Using the Misty mark anywhere other than in a link to the standard, including in an app, on a website, in marketing or on merchandise.
- Calling a modified version of the standard, the tokens or the packages “Misty”. A fork takes a new name that neither contains nor resembles “Misty”, and may say in its description that it’s based on Misty.
- Claiming conformance for a product that doesn’t meet every MUST at the claimed level, or claiming a level that doesn’t exist.
- Words that suggest approval, such as “certified”, “official”, “approved” or “endorsed by ZodiaCALs”. There’s no certification programme.
- Changing the mark in any of the ways ICO-13 prohibits: recolouring, rotating, outlining, adding effects or placing it on a busy photograph.

## How to show the marks

Write the name as “Misty”, in plain text and without changing its form. Where the marks first appear on a page about your product, add “Misty is a trademark of ZodiaCALs.” Until a mark is registered, use ™ rather than ®.

Write to zodiac_phiniphor@outlook.com. Say how you’d like to use the marks, or where you saw them misused.
